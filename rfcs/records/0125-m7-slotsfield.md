Findings:
- the part arm matched every field take first and refused one no literal takes, so the rebuild `s.f.push(v)` never reached `core_alias`, whose doc names that exact case.
The decision, with its count: `s.free.pop()` parses to `let mut s.free[] = s.free`, `s.free[].pop()`, `s.free = s.free[]`, and the core states a read, a `modify` call on the temp and a `Transferred` store back. 38 bodies meet the temp; 33 (`std/slots` `insert<T>`, `remove<T>`) are refused by it alone. The other 5 are element windows (`cells[]` in `cursorSet`, 4; `people[]` in `slots` `main`) with other refusals. The core states a store window into its path (`Builder::nested_store`) and cannot state a call window the same way, because a call's argument is a `Val`, not a place. There are two ways to build it:
- (A) The emitter reads the temp as the place it moved out of: `core_alias` admits a written alias and `core_args_readable` passes `modify` through one, which is the case `m7-falias` left out on purpose. It is sound only because the desugar keeps every other read of `s.free` out of the window, which `ast::is_place_temp` names and `movecheck` exempts by name, and which no kernel judgment states.
- (B) The core states the call on its path, as `nested_store` does for a store: an argument that is a place. That is a change to `Rhs::Call`.
Left: the move-out window around a call (38 bodies, 33 alone), blocked by the choice between (A) and (B); the 5 element windows, blocked by their other refusals as well.
