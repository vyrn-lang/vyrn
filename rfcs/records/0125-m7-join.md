#### A join temporary is a slot its first store takes, and the rename holds it (2026-09-24, `m7-join`)
RFC-0125, milestone M7.
Decision: `Fn_::core_joins` states once what a layout join temporary is: a name the naming pass minted, that no `let` binds and each branch stores whole. The name clause admits it, `core_stmts` slots it at its first store (`core_slot`, as a made layout's `let`), and `core_renames` lets `let v = t` take that slot with no copy, heap or not. The lead's rule (brief `m7-join`). A second rule went first, in its own commit: a rebuild's result takes its receiver's slot only when no other store writes the receiver whole (`core_restored`).
The count, with the measure-walk instrument on `e3e6023a`, over the 758 refused bodies: 129 meet a join temporary, 99 of them refused for it and its rename alone. 32 meet a rename of a sum parameter, each `@t2 = self` in an `Owned__*__release`, and 31 of those are also refused at their `match` by `is_release`. That is the receiver copied before a declared release takes it apart, a separate rule, not this one.
Went: the join and the rename refusals for every layout join. Stayed: 22 bodies whose join is Unit, because the core walk has no reader for a Unit value: `core_val_readable`, the `let` arm and the store arm each ask for a scalar or a layout.
